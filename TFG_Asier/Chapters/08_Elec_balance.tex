\chapter[Electrical Power Balance and Battery Modelling]
{Electrical Power Balance\\and Battery Modelling}
\label{ch:electrical_battery_analysis}

The \texttt{Energy balance} tab estimates electrical generation, load demand
and battery response from the transient thermal results. The absorbed solar
load of the selected panel group is converted into electrical power, while the
internal dissipations of the selected equipment provide an approximation of
the electrical demand. Their difference determines the surplus available for
battery charging or the deficit to be covered during discharge.

The calculation is performed after the \acrshort{esatan-tms} solution has
been completed. The module reads $Q_S$, $Q_I$ and the required temperature
time series from the imported \texttt{.TMD} files, but it neither modifies the
thermal results nor returns electrical values to the thermal model. The data
flow is therefore one-way: the thermal simulation supplies the inputs used by
the electrical balance and battery calculation.

The tab contains two connected areas. The first calculates solar generation,
electrical demand and net power. The second uses the resulting surplus and
deficit profiles, together with the selected battery temperature and model
parameters, to determine stored energy, state of charge, terminal voltage,
current, losses and operating restrictions. Solar-panel, load and battery
groups are selected from the hierarchy of each imported model, so the
calculation is not tied to fixed component names.


\section{Solar Generation and Electrical Power Balance}
\label{sec:solar_electrical_balance}

\subsection{Solar-to-Electrical Conversion and Load Demand}
\label{subsec:solar_conversion_load_demand}

The selected solar-panel group provides the absorbed direct-solar load
$Q_S(t)$ calculated by \acrshort{esatan-tms}. If $Q_{S,i}(t)$ denotes the
incident solar power on the selected nodes and $\alpha$ is their solar
absorptivity, the thermal result satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    Q_S(t)=\alpha Q_{S,i}(t).
    \label{eq:solar_absorbed_power_ch8}
\end{equation}
The application reconstructs the incident contribution as
\begin{equation}
    Q_{S,i}(t)=\frac{Q_S(t)}{\alpha},
    \label{eq:solar_incident_power_ch8}
\end{equation}
and estimates the electrical power generated by the cells from
\begin{equation}
    Q_{S,\mathrm{elec}}(t)
    =
    \eta(t)Q_{S,i}(t)
    =
    \frac{\eta(t)}{\alpha}Q_S(t),
    \label{eq:solar_electrical_power_ch8}
\end{equation}
where $\eta(t)$ is the electrical conversion efficiency. A residual
post-processed coefficient is also calculated as
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_{\mathrm{th}}(t)=\alpha-\eta(t).
    \label{eq:solar_effective_thermal_absorptivity_ch8}
\end{equation}
This coefficient is retained only to show the fraction remaining after the
electrical estimate. It does not replace the absorptivity used by
\acrshort{esatan-tms}, modify the solved value of $Q_S$ or feed any result back
into the thermal model. The admissible range is therefore restricted to
$0<\eta(t)<\alpha\leq1$.

The efficiency may be introduced as a constant or as an expression of the
mean temperature of the selected solar-panel group:
\begin{equation}
    \eta(t)=f_{\eta}\!\left[T_{\mathrm{SP,mean}}(t)\right].
    \label{eq:solar_efficiency_temperature_ch8}
\end{equation}
This option represents the temperature sensitivity of the conversion without
introducing a complete current--voltage or maximum-power-point model. The
expression is evaluated at every output instant, so changes in the panel
temperature are transferred directly to $Q_{S,\mathrm{elec}}$.

The electrical demand is estimated from the positive internal dissipations of
the selected load groups. For a set $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{load}}$, the
application calculates
\begin{equation}
    Q_{\mathrm{load}}(t)
    =
    \sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{load}}}
    \max\!\left[Q_{I,g}(t),0\right].
    \label{eq:electrical_load_ch8}
\end{equation}
For the selected electronic units, $Q_I$ represents the prescribed internal
dissipation associated with operation and is therefore used as an estimate of
the corresponding electrical demand. The result is not a complete spacecraft
power budget and does not separately include converter losses, wiring losses
or power exported from the selected units. A hierarchy parent and one of its
children must not be selected simultaneously when both contain the same nodes,
since their contributions would be added twice.

The balance before battery operation is
\begin{equation}
    P_{\mathrm{net}}(t)
    =
    Q_{S,\mathrm{elec}}(t)-Q_{\mathrm{load}}(t).
    \label{eq:net_electrical_power_ch8}
\end{equation}
The solar contribution supplies the load first. The remaining non-negative
surplus and deficit are therefore
\begin{align}
    P_{\mathrm{sur}}(t)
    &=
    \max\!\left[P_{\mathrm{net}}(t),0\right],
    \label{eq:solar_surplus_ch8}
    \\
    P_{\mathrm{def}}(t)
    &=
    \max\!\left[-P_{\mathrm{net}}(t),0\right].
    \label{eq:electrical_deficit_ch8}
\end{align}
Only $P_{\mathrm{sur}}$ can charge the battery, while
$P_{\mathrm{def}}$ defines the demand to be covered during discharge.

\section{Battery Model}
\label{sec:battery_model}

The battery calculation starts from the solar-generation and load-demand
profiles obtained in the first area of the tab. At each interval, solar power
supplies the selected load first. Any remaining surplus is offered to the
battery, whereas a remaining deficit requests battery discharge. The accepted
power depends on stored energy, terminal-voltage limits, internal resistance,
charge and discharge efficiencies, charge-control parameters and the battery
temperature obtained from the thermal solution.

The battery is recalculated whenever an upstream electrical input or any
battery parameter changes. Consequently, the same pack configuration may
produce a different response after changing the selected panel group, the
load elements, $\alpha$, the solar-efficiency expression or the imported
thermal case.


\subsection{Energy-Based Formulation and Model Scope}
\label{subsec:battery_theoretical_basis}

The discharged-energy level $\phi$ represents the energy removed from a fully
charged battery and is used as the main state variable. This energy-based
description relates the battery state directly to the exchanged electrical
power instead of relying only on discharged ampere-hours. Thermal-vacuum tests
reported charge and discharge energy efficiencies between approximately
97.5 and 99 percent for the characterised lithium-ion battery
\cite{porras2018battery}.

During discharge, the state evolves as
\begin{equation}
    \phi(t)
    =
    \phi_0
    +
    \int_{t_0}^{t}
    \left[V I_d+R_d I_d^2\right]\mathrm{d}t,
    \label{eq:battery_discharge_energy_ch8}
\end{equation}
whereas charging reduces the discharged-energy level according to
\begin{equation}
    \phi(t)
    =
    \phi_0
    -
    \int_{t_0}^{t}
    \left[V I_c-R_c I_c^2\right]\mathrm{d}t.
    \label{eq:battery_charge_energy_ch8}
\end{equation}
Here, $V$ is the terminal voltage, $I_d$ and $I_c$ are non-negative
discharge and charge current magnitudes, and $R_d$ and $R_c$ are the
corresponding internal resistances. The equivalent-circuit terminal voltages
are
\begin{align}
    V_d(\phi,I_d)
    &=
    E_d(\phi)-R_d I_d,
    \label{eq:battery_discharge_voltage_ch8}
    \\
    V_c(\phi,I_c)
    &=
    E_c(\phi)+R_c I_c.
    \label{eq:battery_charge_voltage_ch8}
\end{align}
The internal-voltage functions may be identified from measured charge and
discharge curves, while resistor--capacitor branches can be added to represent
dynamic behaviour \cite{porras2018battery}. No cell-specific calibration data
are available for the configurable pack used here. The application therefore
retains the discharged-energy state, separate charge and discharge
resistances, and the terminal-voltage relations, but uses a single generic
piecewise-linear internal-voltage curve $E(\phi)$ for both operating
directions. The curve is defined from the selected cut-off, nominal and charge
voltage limits, and no dynamic resistor--capacitor branches are included.

Let $C_{\mathrm{pack,Wh}}$ denote the nominal energy capacity and
$E_{\mathrm{st}}(t)$ the stored energy. The internal state used by the
application is
\begin{equation}
    \phi(t)
    =
    C_{\mathrm{pack,Wh}}-E_{\mathrm{st}}(t),
    \label{eq:battery_phi_implemented_ch8}
\end{equation}
and the reported state of charge is
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{SOC}(t)
    =
    100\,
    \frac{E_{\mathrm{st}}(t)}{C_{\mathrm{pack,Wh}}}.
    \label{eq:battery_soc_ch8}
\end{equation}
At each numerical substep, the current is obtained from $P=VI$ using the
current value of $\phi$ and the corresponding terminal-voltage relation. The
stored energy is updated from the internal power $E(\phi)I$, while the
difference between internal and terminal power is reported as resistive loss.
Separate conversion efficiencies account for losses between the solar surplus
and the battery terminals during charge and between the battery terminals and
the load during discharge.

The formulation provides an engineering estimate for the selected inputs. It
is not a qualified flight-battery model and does not replace cell-specific
testing, pack-level verification or mission-dependent operating limits. Such
limits must be established for the selected technology and environment
\cite{ecss_hb_20_02a,mckissock2009liion}.


\subsection{Pack Configuration and Charge--Discharge Control}
\label{subsec:battery_configuration_control}

The first battery block connects the model with the imported thermal result
and defines the pack configuration. The \emph{Battery TMD element} supplies
the mean battery temperature. This temperature is used by the charge-derating
function and by efficiency expressions containing $T$.

Figure~\ref{fig:battery_thermal_pack_inputs_ch8} shows the corresponding
controls. The lower and upper charge-temperature limits define the interval in
which charging is permitted, while the ramp margin introduces a linear
transition near each limit.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[
        width=0.86\textwidth,
        keepaspectratio
    ]{Figures/08/battery_pack_configuration.png}
    \caption{Battery-temperature source, charge derating and cell-pack
    configuration.}
    \label{fig:battery_thermal_pack_inputs_ch8}
\end{figure}

\begin{samepage}
The selected configuration uses the \texttt{BATTERY} thermal group, charge
limits of \(0\) and \(45\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\), and a \(5\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\)
derating margin. The pack contains six cells in series and one parallel string.
Each cell has a charge capacity of \(3\,\mathrm{Ah}\), a cut-off voltage of
\(3.0\,\mathrm{V}\), a nominal voltage of \(3.7\,\mathrm{V}\) and a charge
limit of \(4.1\,\mathrm{V}\). This gives pack limits of \(18.0\), \(22.2\)
and \(24.6\,\mathrm{V}\), a nominal energy capacity of \(66.6\,\mathrm{Wh}\),
and an initial SOC of \(60\%\).

For a ramp margin $m_T$ that does not cause the lower and upper transitions to
overlap, the charge-derating factor is
\begin{equation}
    f_T(T)
    =
    \begin{cases}
        0,
        & T\leq T_{\mathrm{low}},
        \\[0.35em]
        \dfrac{T-T_{\mathrm{low}}}{m_T},
        & T_{\mathrm{low}}<T<T_{\mathrm{low}}+m_T,
        \\[0.9em]
        1,
        & T_{\mathrm{low}}+m_T
          \leq T\leq
          T_{\mathrm{high}}-m_T,
        \\[0.35em]
        \dfrac{T_{\mathrm{high}}-T}{m_T},
        & T_{\mathrm{high}}-m_T<T<T_{\mathrm{high}},
        \\[0.9em]
        0,
        & T\geq T_{\mathrm{high}}.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:battery_charge_derating_ch8}
\end{equation}
The factor multiplies both the maximum charge current and the maximum charge
power. A value of one permits normal charging, a value of zero blocks it, and
intermediate values reduce the accepted surplus. Discharge is not derated by
this factor.
\end{samepage}

The cell arrangement contains $N_s$ cells in series and $N_p$ parallel
strings. The pack voltages are
\begin{equation}
    V_{x,\mathrm{pack}}
    =
    N_s V_{x,\mathrm{cell}},
    \qquad
    x\in\{\mathrm{cut},\mathrm{nom},\mathrm{chg}\},
    \label{eq:battery_pack_voltages_ch8}
\end{equation}
while the pack capacities become
\begin{align}
    C_{\mathrm{pack,Ah}}
    &=
    N_p C_{\mathrm{cell,Ah}},
    \label{eq:battery_pack_capacity_ah_ch8}
    \\
    C_{\mathrm{pack,Wh}}
    &=
    V_{\mathrm{nom,pack}}C_{\mathrm{pack,Ah}}.
    \label{eq:battery_pack_capacity_wh_ch8}
\end{align}
The equivalent charge and discharge resistances follow
\begin{equation}
    R_{x,\mathrm{pack}}
    =
    R_{x,\mathrm{cell}}\frac{N_s}{N_p},
    \qquad
    x\in\{c,d\}.
    \label{eq:battery_pack_resistance_ch8}
\end{equation}
The initial SOC sets the stored energy at the first output instant:
\begin{equation}
    E_{\mathrm{st},0}
    =
    \frac{\mathrm{SOC}_0}{100}C_{\mathrm{pack,Wh}}.
    \label{eq:battery_initial_energy_ch8}
\end{equation}
The second block defines the electrical efficiencies, terminal-power limits,
internal resistances and simplified CC--CV control. Figure~\ref{fig:battery_electrical_inputs_ch8}
shows this area of the interface.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[
        width=0.86\textwidth,
        keepaspectratio
    ]{Figures/08/battery_electrical_parameters.png}
    \caption{Battery efficiencies, power limits, internal resistances and
    simplified CC--CV charge-control parameters.}
    \label{fig:battery_electrical_inputs_ch8}
\end{figure}

The nominal calculation uses constant charge and discharge efficiencies of
\(0.98\), maximum charge and discharge powers of \(40\,\mathrm{W}\), and
cell resistances of \(0.01667\,\Omega\) in both operating directions. The
resulting pack resistances are \(0.10\,\Omega\). The CC--CV parameters are a
maximum charge rate of \(0.5C\), a current-reduction transition at
\(85\%\) SOC, a taper exponent of \(1.5\), and a charge-complete current
threshold of \(0.10\,\mathrm{A}\).

Charge and discharge efficiencies may be constants or functions of battery
temperature and terminal voltage:
\begin{equation}
    \eta_c(t)
    =
    f_c\!\left[T_{\mathrm{batt}}(t),V(t)\right],
    \qquad
    \eta_d(t)
    =
    f_d\!\left[T_{\mathrm{batt}}(t),V(t)\right].
    \label{eq:battery_efficiency_functions_ch8}
\end{equation}
During charge, $\eta_c$ determines the fraction of the available solar
surplus that reaches the battery terminals. During discharge, $\eta_d$
determines the fraction of terminal discharge power delivered to the load.
The remaining contribution is reported as conversion loss. The internal
resistive loss for each operating direction is
\begin{equation}
    P_{\mathrm{loss,int}}(t)=R_x I_x^2(t),
    \qquad x\in\{c,d\}.
    \label{eq:battery_internal_loss_ch8}
\end{equation}
The maximum charge and discharge powers provide independent terminal-power
limits. During the constant-current part of charging, the current ceiling is
\begin{equation}
    I_{\mathrm{CC,max}}
    =
    C_{\mathrm{rate}}C_{\mathrm{pack,Ah}},
    \label{eq:battery_cc_current_limit_ch8}
\end{equation}
and is reduced by the thermal factor from
Equation~\eqref{eq:battery_charge_derating_ch8}. The terminal voltage must
also satisfy
\begin{equation}
    E(\phi)+R_cI_c
    \leq
    V_{\mathrm{chg,pack}}.
    \label{eq:battery_cv_voltage_limit_ch8}
\end{equation}
Above the selected transition value $\mathrm{SOC}_{\mathrm{CV}}$, an
additional taper factor reduces the charge-current limit:
\begin{equation}
    f_{\mathrm{CV}}(\mathrm{SOC})
    =
    \left[
        \frac{100-\mathrm{SOC}}
        {100-\mathrm{SOC}_{\mathrm{CV}}}
    \right]^{\gamma},
    \qquad
    \mathrm{SOC}\geq\mathrm{SOC}_{\mathrm{CV}},
    \label{eq:battery_cv_taper_ch8}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma$ controls the reduction shape. Charging is considered complete
when the resulting current falls below the selected threshold in the upper
SOC region. Together with the terminal-voltage limit, this SOC-based taper
provides a simplified approximation of constant-current and constant-voltage
charging; it does not represent a dedicated charger circuit
\cite{ti2022charger}. Any surplus that cannot be accepted because of power,
current, voltage, temperature or capacity restrictions is reported as
curtailed solar power.

During discharge, the current is limited by the available stored energy, the
maximum discharge power and the pack cut-off voltage. Any remaining deficit
is reported as unserved load. These variables make the consequences of an
undersized battery or restrictive operating limit visible without modifying
the upstream thermal results.
