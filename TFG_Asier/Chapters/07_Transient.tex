\chapter[Transient Thermal Analysis]
{Transient Thermal Analysis}
\label{ch:transient_analysis}

The multi-case structure developed in
Chapter~\ref{ch:multicase_tool} provides the model hierarchy, case collection,
time controls and representation options used by the analysis modules. Within
this framework, the \texttt{TRANSIENT} tab processes the time-dependent
temperatures, thermal loads and conductor data stored in the imported
\acrshort{esatan-tms} \texttt{.TMD} files. The original simulation results
remain unchanged, and all additional calculations are performed during
post-processing.

The nominal case \texttt{SAT\_AFM\_TRANSITORIO.TMD} is used to
illustrate temperature and thermal-load evolution, interface temperature differences, variations over
selected time windows, summary values, conductive and radiative heat flows,
and accumulated thermal energy. Comparisons between different environmental
and operating cases are reserved for the results chapter.

\section{Temperature and Thermal-Load Evolution}
\label{sec:thermal_evolution}

The first area provides a general view of the transient response through the
evolution of temperatures, thermal loads and selected group attributes. To
build these time-dependent representations, the application uses the Excel
hierarchy to associate each physical group \(g\) with its corresponding set of
nodes \(\mathcal{N}_g\).

At every output instant, the minimum, mean and maximum temperatures of the
group are calculated as:
\begin{align}
    T_{g,\min}(t)
    &=
    \min\!\left[ T_n(t) \right]_{n\in\mathcal{N}_g}
    \label{eq:transient_group_tmin}
    \\
    T_{g,\mathrm{mean}}(t)
    &=
    \frac{1}{|\mathcal{N}_g|}
    \sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}_g} T_n(t)
    \label{eq:transient_group_tmean}
    \\
    T_{g,\max}(t)
    &=
    \max\!\left[ T_n(t) \right]_{n\in\mathcal{N}_g}
    \label{eq:transient_group_tmax}
\end{align}
Nodal thermal-load contributions are additive, so they are combined through
a different aggregation rule. For any available contribution \(Q_x\), the
corresponding group evolution is obtained from:
\begin{equation}
    Q_{x,g}(t)
    =
    \sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}_g} Q_{x,n}(t),
    \qquad
    x\in\{A,E,S,I,R\}.
    \label{eq:transient_group_load}
\end{equation}
Here, \(Q_A\), \(Q_E\) and \(Q_S\) denote the absorbed albedo, planetary
infrared and direct-solar loads, respectively, while \(Q_I\) and \(Q_R\)
represent the internal and additional applied loads exported with the solved
model. Together with the temperature statistics defined above, these aggregated
series form the basis for the subsequent analyses available in
the tab.

The configuration begins with the selection of one or more cases and model
elements. For each element, the user then chooses the attributes to be
represented. The available options are \(T_{\max}\), \(T_{\mathrm{mean}}\),
\(T_{\min}\), \(Q_A\), \(Q_E\), \(Q_S\), \(Q_I\), \(Q_R\), \(C\) and
\(A\). The three temperature statistics are activated by default, although 
the user can select any other combination.

The temperature statistics describe the thermal state of the selected
element. \(T_{\mathrm{mean}}\) represents its overall evolution, while
\(T_{\max}\) and \(T_{\min}\) identify the highest and lowest nodal
temperatures at each output instant. The distance between both limits defines
the instantaneous temperature range within the corresponding group.

The thermal-load terms can be represented separately or together with the
temperature statistics. Their curves show how the environmental and
internally applied loads acting on the selected element vary throughout the
simulation. When an element contains several nodes, the displayed value
corresponds to the group sum defined in
Equation~\eqref{eq:transient_group_load}.

Thermal capacitance \(C\) and surface area \(A\) can also be displayed. Unlike
the temperatures and loads, these properties remain constant throughout the
transient simulation and are therefore not used in the time-dependent
examples presented below.

The display units are configured independently from the attribute selection.
The temperature unit is fixed to the value stored in the loaded
\texttt{.TMD} file, while thermal loads can be represented in watts or
milliwatts and elapsed time in seconds, minutes, hours, days, months or
years. Further controls modify the horizontal-axis label, graph width and height,
axis tick font size and legend font size.

The interface also provides the multi-case representation alternative introduced
in Section~\ref{subsec:multicase_representation_options}. The examples
presented here use a single case, since this is sufficient to explain the
functionality of this area of the tab.

\section{Interface Temperature-Difference Analysis}
\label{sec:interface_temperature_analysis}

The second area examines the temperature difference between two selected
groups. The interface is defined by a source group and a target group, and
several source--target pairs may be added to the same analysis. One or more
cases can then be evaluated using the resulting interface selection.

The target list may be restricted to groups connected to the source through
explicit GL or GR conductors. This filter limits the selection to interfaces
represented directly in the thermal network. The user can add one
target at a time or include all the connections detected for the chosen
source.

Two calculation bases are available, together with an optional limit for the
maximum permitted temperature difference.
Figure~\ref{fig:interface_analysis_configuration} shows the controls used to
select the cases, source and target groups, calculation method and acceptance
limit.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[
        width=1.0\textwidth,
        keepaspectratio
    ]{Figures/07/display09.png}
    \caption{Configuration area for selecting cases, source and target
    groups, the interface calculation basis and the optional
    temperature-difference limit.}
    \label{fig:interface_analysis_configuration}
\end{figure}

The default calculation basis uses the node pairs connected through explicit
GL or GR conductors. Let \(\mathcal{P}_{AB}\) contain the pairs \((i,j)\)
linking source group \(A\) with target group \(B\). The absolute temperature
difference associated with each pair is
\begin{equation}
    \Delta T_{ij}(t)
    =
    \left|T_i(t)-T_j(t)\right|
    \label{eq:if_pair_delta_t}
\end{equation}
At every output instant, the interface value corresponds to the largest
difference among the connected pairs:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta T_{A,B}^{\mathrm{I/F}}(t)
    =
    \max\!\left[
        \Delta T_{ij}(t)
    \right]_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{P}_{AB}}
    \label{eq:if_max_delta_t}
\end{equation}
The maximum difference reached throughout the complete simulation is then
\begin{equation}
    \Delta T_{A,B,\max}^{\mathrm{I/F}}
    =
    \max\!\left[
        \Delta T_{A,B}^{\mathrm{I/F}}(t)
    \right]_{t}
    \label{eq:if_absolute_max_delta_t}
\end{equation}
At each output instant, the tool represents the hotter and colder
temperatures of the connected pair producing the largest absolute difference.
This pair may change throughout the simulation. A vertical marker identifies
the global maximum, and the summary output records the nodes and coupling
associated with that event.

The example presented below uses \texttt{OBC\_BOX} as the source and
\texttt{RADIATOR} as the target. This interface is representative because the
thermal network includes an additional conductive link between both elements,
as described in Section~\ref{sec:thermal_network_definition}.
Figure~\ref{fig:nominal_obc_radiator_interface} shows that the maximum
temperature difference reaches \(20.8\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\) at
\(3388.1\,\mathrm{s}\).

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[
        width=1.0\textwidth,
        keepaspectratio
    ]{Figures/07/nominal_obc_radiator_interface_temperature.png}
    \caption{Hot and cold temperatures of the connected pair producing the
    largest interface difference at each output instant between
    \texttt{OBC\_BOX} and \texttt{RADIATOR}. The vertical marker identifies
    the global maximum in the nominal transient case.}
    \label{fig:nominal_obc_radiator_interface}
\end{figure}

All targets connected to the same source group through explicit conductors
were then added. For each source--target pair,
Table~\ref{tab:nominal_obc_interfaces} records the maximum absolute

The alternative calculation basis uses the complete temperature envelopes of
the source and target groups. It does not require the nodes producing the
result to be connected by an explicit conductor. The corresponding difference is
defined as
\begin{equation}
    \Delta T_{A,B}^{\mathrm{env}}(t)
    =
    \max\!\left[
        \left|T_{A,\max}(t)-T_{B,\min}(t)\right|,
        \left|T_{B,\max}(t)-T_{A,\min}(t)\right|
    \right]
    \label{eq:if_group_envelope}
\end{equation}
This method provides a more conservative comparison between the complete
groups, but the nodes defining the result do not necessarily represent a
physical interface. The conductor-based calculation therefore offers greater
traceability whenever a direct GL or GR connection is available.

A maximum permitted difference \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{lim}}\) can also be
introduced. The available margin is calculated from
\begin{equation}
    M_{\mathrm{I/F}}
    =
    \Delta T_{\mathrm{lim}}
    -
    \Delta T_{\max}
    \label{eq:if_margin}
\end{equation}
where \(\Delta T_{\max}\) denotes the maximum value obtained with the selected
calculation basis. A non-negative margin produces a \texttt{PASS} result,
whereas a negative value produces \texttt{FAIL}. Setting
\(\Delta T_{\mathrm{lim}}\) to zero disables this criterion, as in the
nominal example presented above.

\section{Temperature Variation over Selected Time Windows}
\label{sec:temperature_time_windows}

The third area evaluates how much the temperature of each node changes over a
selected time window. This representation complements the absolute
temperatures examined in Section~\ref{sec:thermal_evolution}, since a
component may remain within its operating limits while still undergoing a
significant increase or decrease during a shorter interval.

The configuration starts with the selection of one or more cases, model
elements and time windows. Several windows can be processed simultaneously,
and the resulting variations may be represented through their maximum and
minimum nodal values or supplemented with the corresponding mean curve. The
elapsed-time unit, graph dimensions, axis labels and font sizes can also be
modified. Two optional limits evaluate the maximum absolute temperature change
and its average magnitude over the selected interval.

Figure~\ref{fig:delta_t_window_configuration} shows the controls used to
define the cases, elements, time windows, representation method and acceptance
criteria.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[
        width=1.0\textwidth,
        keepaspectratio
    ]{Figures/07/display10.png}
    \caption{Configuration area for selecting the cases, model elements,
    time windows, representation method and optional temperature-variation
    limits.}
    \label{fig:delta_t_window_configuration}
\end{figure}

For a requested window \(\tau\), the temperature variation of node \(n\) is
calculated from
\begin{equation}
    \Delta T_n(t;\tau)
    =
    T_n(t)-T_n(t-\tau^{*})
    \label{eq:window_delta_t}
\end{equation}
where \(\tau^{*}\) is the effective interval reproduced by the output-time
vector. When the simulation uses a uniform output step \(\Delta t_m\), the
number of samples required for the calculation is
\begin{equation}
    k_{\tau}
    =
    \left\lceil
        \frac{\tau}{\Delta t_m}
    \right\rceil
    \qquad
    \tau^{*}=k_{\tau}\Delta t_m
    \label{eq:effective_delta_t_window}
\end{equation}
The ceiling operation prevents the effective interval from being shorter than
the requested value. If both values differ, the interface reports
\(\tau^{*}\) together with the selected window.

For a group \(g\), the maximum--minimum representation is obtained from the
nodal variations:
\begin{align}
    \Delta T_{g,\min}(t;\tau)
    &=
    \min\!\left[
        \Delta T_n(t;\tau)
    \right]_{n\in\mathcal{N}_g}
    \label{eq:window_group_delta_t_min}
    \\
    \Delta T_{g,\max}(t;\tau)
    &=
    \max\!\left[
        \Delta T_n(t;\tau)
    \right]_{n\in\mathcal{N}_g}
    \label{eq:window_group_delta_t_max}
\end{align}
Positive values indicate that the node has warmed during the preceding
interval, whereas negative values indicate cooling. The separation between
both curves shows whether the nodes of the selected element follow a similar
evolution or experience different temperature changes.

The nominal example uses \texttt{BODY\_PANEL} and requested windows of
10 and 30 minutes. This element contains several nodes with sufficiently
different responses to make the maximum--minimum envelopes visible.
The effective intervals obtained from the available output grid are
approximately \(10.084\) and \(30.251\) minutes.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[
        width=1.0\textwidth,
        keepaspectratio
    ]{Figures/07/nominal_body_panel_deltaT_windows.png}
    \caption{Maximum and minimum temperature variations of
    \texttt{BODY\_PANEL} over 10- and 30-minute windows in the nominal
    transient case.}
    \label{fig:nominal_body_panel_delta_t_windows}
\end{figure}

The longer window produces a wider envelope because it accumulates the
temperature change over a greater part of the orbital cycle. However, the
largest absolute variation does not by itself indicate how rapidly the change
occurs. For this purpose, the application also calculates the average
magnitude over the effective interval:
\begin{equation}
    R_{T,n}(t;\tau)
    =
    \frac{
        \left|\Delta T_n(t;\tau)\right|
    }{
        \tau^{*}
    }
    \label{eq:window_average_rate}
\end{equation}
This metric represents an average change over the selected window rather
than an instantaneous derivative. Its value therefore depends on the duration
of the interval.

Table~\ref{tab:nominal_body_panel_deltaT_windows} summarises the critical
results obtained for both windows. It includes the minimum and maximum
temperature changes, their event times and nodes, the largest absolute
variation and its corresponding average magnitude.

\input{Tables/table_nominal_body_panel_deltaT_windows.tex}
\NominalBodyPanelDeltaTTable[1.0\textwidth]

The 30-minute interval reaches the largest absolute variation,
\(2.23\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\), whereas the 10-minute interval produces the
higher average magnitude, \(0.095\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C/min}\). The two results
therefore describe different aspects of the transient response: the longer
window identifies the greater accumulated change, while the shorter one is
more sensitive to rapid variations.

The complete nodal values can also be displayed and exported from the
interface. Optional limits for \(\left|\Delta T\right|\) and
\(\left|\Delta T\right|/\tau^{*}\) allow margins and acceptance states to be
calculated. A value of zero disables the corresponding criterion, as in the
example presented here.

\section{Transient Thermal Summary Table}
\label{sec:thermal_summary_table}

The fourth area condenses the transient temperature results of the selected
elements into a single tabular output. Instead of representing their complete
evolution, it identifies the main temperature extrema and the largest and
smallest spatial temperature ranges reached during the simulation. This
provides an initial overview of the most relevant values before examining a
specific element in greater detail.

The configuration requires one or more cases and model elements. The case
representation selector also enables the direct and difference-based options
introduced in Section~\ref{subsec:multicase_representation_options}. The
nominal example uses a single case, since this is sufficient to explain the
content and organisation of the resulting table.

Figure~\ref{fig:thermal_summary_configuration} shows the selection area of
this function.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[
        width=1.0\textwidth,
        keepaspectratio
    ]{Figures/07/display11.png}
    \caption[Transient thermal-summary configuration.]{Configuration area for
    selecting the cases and model elements included in the transient thermal
    summary.}
    \label{fig:thermal_summary_configuration}
\end{figure}

Let \(\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{out}}\) denote the set of output instants contained
in the transient result file. The global maximum and minimum temperatures of
a group \(g\) are calculated as:
\begin{align}
    T_{g,\max}^{\mathrm{glob}}
    &=
    \max\!\left[
        T_n(t)
    \right]_{
        \substack{
            n\in\mathcal{N}_g\\
            t\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{out}}
        }
    }
    \label{eq:summary_absolute_tmax}
    \\
    T_{g,\min}^{\mathrm{glob}}
    &=
    \min\!\left[
        T_n(t)
    \right]_{
        \substack{
            n\in\mathcal{N}_g\\
            t\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{out}}
        }
    }
    \label{eq:summary_absolute_tmin}
\end{align}
For each extreme, the output also identifies the node and simulation time at
which it occurs. These data allow the critical value to be located without
inspecting the temperature curves of all the nodes individually.

The table also evaluates the spatial temperature range of each group at every
output instant:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta T_g^{\mathrm{sp}}(t)
    =
    T_{g,\max}(t)-T_{g,\min}(t)
    \label{eq:summary_spatial_delta_t}
\end{equation}
Its maximum and minimum values throughout the simulation are then obtained
from:
\begin{align}
    \Delta T_{g,\max}^{\mathrm{sp}}
    &=
    \max\!\left[
        \Delta T_g^{\mathrm{sp}}(t)
    \right]_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{out}}}
    \label{eq:summary_spatial_delta_t_max}
    \\
    \Delta T_{g,\min}^{\mathrm{sp}}
    &=
    \min\!\left[
        \Delta T_g^{\mathrm{sp}}(t)
    \right]_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{out}}}
    \label{eq:summary_spatial_delta_t_min}
\end{align}
The corresponding times and hot--cold node pairs are retained in the output.
This calculation differs from the time-window method of
Section~\ref{sec:temperature_time_windows}. There,
\(\Delta T_n(t;\tau)\) compares one node at two different instants, whereas
\(\Delta T_g^{\mathrm{sp}}(t)\) compares different nodes of the same element
at one instant.

The original output contains the temperature extrema and spatial ranges in a
single table. For readability in the document, the nominal results are
divided into two complementary tables. The first contains the global maximum
and minimum temperatures, together with their event times and critical nodes,
while the second contains the maximum and
minimum spatial ranges reached by the same elements. The associated node pairs
identify the locations that define each range.

\input{Tables/table_nominal_thermal_summary.tex}
\NominalThermalExtremaTable[0.75\textwidth]

\NominalThermalRangeTable[0.85\textwidth]

The two outputs serve different purposes. Global extrema indicate the highest
and lowest temperatures reached by an element during the complete simulation,
while the spatial ranges describe its internal thermal non-uniformity at a
given instant. Their combined use therefore distinguishes a general shift in
component temperature from a large difference between its individual nodes.

The summary can be generated for any selection of elements and exported for
later inspection. No separate graph is required in this area, since its main
function is to provide a compact numerical screening of the transient
solution.

\section{Conductive and Radiative Heat-Flow Evolution}
\label{sec:heat_flow_evolution}

The fifth function examines the heat transferred between a selected source
and target throughout the transient simulation. After choosing one or more
cases, the user defines both elements and selects the conductive,
radiative or total heat-flow components to be represented. The output can be
expressed in watts or milliwatts, while the horizontal axis uses the selected
elapsed-time unit.

Figure~\ref{fig:heat_flow_configuration} shows the corresponding controls.
The three available components are \(Q_{\mathrm{GL}}\),
\(Q_{\mathrm{GR}}\) and \(Q_{\mathrm{GT}}\), representing the linear
conductive, radiative and combined exchanges, respectively. Their sign follows
the source--target order defined in the interface.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[
        width=1.0\textwidth,
        keepaspectratio
    ]{Figures/07/display12.png}
    \caption{Configuration area for selecting the cases, source and target
    elements, heat-flow components and display units.}
    \label{fig:heat_flow_configuration}
\end{figure}

Let \(\mathcal{P}_{AB}^{\mathrm{GL}}\) contain the node pairs connected by
linear conductors between source \(A\) and target \(B\). The conductive
contribution is calculated as
\begin{equation}
    Q_{\mathrm{GL},A\leftarrow B}(t)
    =
    \sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{P}_{AB}^{\mathrm{GL}}}
    \mathrm{GL}_{ij}
    \left[T_j(t)-T_i(t)\right]
    \label{eq:transient_gl_flow}
\end{equation}
where node \(i\) belongs to the source and node \(j\) to the target. The
radiative contribution is obtained from the pairs contained in
\(\mathcal{P}_{AB}^{\mathrm{GR}}\):
\begin{equation}
    Q_{\mathrm{GR},A\leftarrow B}(t)
    =
    \sigma
    \sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{P}_{AB}^{\mathrm{GR}}}
    \mathrm{GR}_{ij}
    \left[
        T_{j,K}^{4}(t)-T_{i,K}^{4}(t)
    \right]
    \label{eq:transient_gr_flow}
\end{equation}
The temperatures are converted to kelvin before evaluating the fourth-power
term. The total exchange combines both mechanisms:
\begin{equation}
    Q_{\mathrm{GT},A\leftarrow B}(t)
    =
    Q_{\mathrm{GL},A\leftarrow B}(t)
    +
    Q_{\mathrm{GR},A\leftarrow B}(t)
    \label{eq:transient_total_flow}
\end{equation}
With this ordering, a positive value represents heat entering the selected
source from the target. A negative value indicates heat leaving the source
towards the target. Reversing the source and target therefore reverses the
sign of the represented exchange.

The nominal example uses \texttt{BATTERY} as the source and
\texttt{BODY\_PLATE\_INF} as the target. The three heat-flow components are
represented in watts over the complete simulation.
Figure~\ref{fig:nominal_battery_body_plate_heat_flow} shows that all values
remain negative, indicating a continuous transfer from the battery towards
its supporting plate.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[
        width=0.8\textwidth,
        keepaspectratio
    ]{Figures/07/nominal_battery_body_plate_inf_heat_flow.png}
    \caption{Conductive, radiative and total heat flows between
    \texttt{BATTERY} and \texttt{BODY\_PLATE\_INF} in the nominal transient
    case. Negative values indicate heat leaving the battery.}
    \label{fig:nominal_battery_body_plate_heat_flow}
\end{figure}

exchange remains comparatively small. Consequently,
\(Q_{\mathrm{GT}}\) follows the shape of \(Q_{\mathrm{GL}}\), with a
slightly larger outward magnitude. The transfer increases during the eclipse
interval and reaches its most negative value at approximately
\(3388.1\,\mathrm{s}\).

The interface also generates a table containing the value of each
heat-flow component at every output instant. Since the complete export would be unnecessarily
long for the document, Table~
\ref{tab:nominal_battery_body_plate_heat_flow} summarises only the minimum and

\section{Absorbed and Released Thermal Energy}
\label{sec:absorbed_released_energy}

The final area integrates the thermal-power terms associated with a selected
element over time. Its configuration begins with the selection of one or more
cases and a model element. The user then defines the power contributions to be
included and chooses the time, power and energy units used in the
representation.

The available power terms comprise \(Q_A\), \(Q_E\), \(Q_S\), \(Q_I\) and
\(Q_R\), together with the net conductive and radiative exchanges with nodes
outside the selected group, \(Q_{\mathrm{GL,ext}}\) and
\(Q_{\mathrm{GR,ext}}\). The complete thermal balance is also available and
is calculated as
\begin{equation}
    Q_{\mathrm{balance},g}(t)
    =
    Q_{A,g}(t)
    +
    Q_{E,g}(t)
    +
    Q_{S,g}(t)
    +
    Q_{I,g}(t)
    +
    Q_{R,g}(t)
    +
    Q_{\mathrm{GL,ext},g}(t)
    +
    Q_{\mathrm{GR,ext},g}(t)
    \label{eq:component_thermal_balance}
\end{equation}
where the external conductive and radiative terms contain only exchanges
between nodes belonging to group \(g\) and nodes outside that group. Internal
connections between two nodes of the selected element are excluded because
they redistribute energy within the group without changing its total balance.

Figure~\ref{fig:thermal_energy_configuration} shows the controls used to
select the cases, element, power terms and display units. The power curves may
be expressed in watts or milliwatts, while the time-integrated energy is
represented in joules or watt-hours.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[
        width=1.0\textwidth,
        keepaspectratio
    ]{Figures/07/display13.png}
    \caption{Configuration area for selecting the cases, model element,
    thermal-power terms and units used in the energy calculation.}
    \label{fig:thermal_energy_configuration}
\end{figure}

For a selected power term \(Q_{x,g}\), the net energy transferred from the
start of the simulation to time \(t\) is
\begin{equation}
    E_{x,g}(t)
    =
    \int_{t_0}^{t}
    Q_{x,g}(\xi)\,
    \mathrm{d}\xi
    \label{eq:thermal_energy_integral}
\end{equation}
The numerical integration follows the output-time vector contained in the
result file. Between two successive instants, the trapezoidal rule gives
\begin{equation}
    E_{x,g}(t_k)
    =
    E_{x,g}(t_{k-1})
    +
    \frac{
        Q_{x,g}(t_{k-1})
        +
        Q_{x,g}(t_k)
    }{2}
    \left(
        t_k-t_{k-1}
    \right)
    \label{eq:thermal_energy_trapezoidal}
\end{equation}
with \(E_{x,g}(t_0)=0\). When time is expressed in seconds, the resulting
energy is obtained directly in joules. Its conversion to watt-hours follows
\begin{equation}
    E_{x,g}^{\mathrm{Wh}}(t)
    =
    \frac{
        E_{x,g}^{\mathrm{J}}(t)
    }{3600}
    \label{eq:joule_to_watt_hour}
\end{equation}
The sign of the power term determines the evolution of its time-integrated
energy. A positive value represents energy entering the selected group and
increases the integrated value, whereas a negative value represents energy
leaving the group and decreases it. When the power becomes zero, the
corresponding value remains constant. The resulting curve is therefore not
necessarily monotonic and represents the net energy transferred relative to
the initial instant.

The nominal example uses the group \texttt{SOLAR\_PANELS} and represents the
albedo and direct-solar contributions \(Q_A\) and \(Q_S\). Power is expressed
in watts, energy in watt-hours and elapsed time in minutes. Both the
instantaneous power curves and their time-integrated values are displayed in
the same graph using separate vertical axes.

        width=1.0\textwidth,
        keepaspectratio
    ]{Figures/07/nominal_solar_panels_thermal_balance_energy.png}
    \caption{Albedo and direct-solar thermal powers and their time-integrated
    energy contributions for \texttt{SOLAR\_PANELS} in the nominal transient

    \label{fig:nominal_solar_panels_thermal_energy}
\end{figure}

The direct-solar contribution dominates while the spacecraft remains
illuminated and becomes zero during eclipse. Consequently, its integrated
energy increases during the illuminated intervals and remains constant while
direct solar radiation is absent. The albedo contribution is smaller and
varies according to the orbital position, producing a more gradual change in
its integrated value.

The graph illustrates the relation between an instantaneous thermal input and
the net energy transferred over a finite interval.

The same calculation can be applied to individual loads, external heat
exchanges or the complete balance \(Q_{\mathrm{balance}}\). In the latter
case, increases and decreases of the integrated curve identify periods of net
energy absorption and release by the selected element. Provided that all
relevant exchanges are included, the curve represents the change in thermal
energy relative to the initial instant, rather than the absolute energy stored
in the element.

These results describe thermal power and thermal energy obtained from the
\acrshort{esatan-tms} solution. They do not represent the electrical energy
generated by the solar cells. The conversion of the incident solar load into
electrical power and its use within the battery model are treated separately
in the electrical-balance analysis.
